What a Nikaah look is built for

Nikaah photography is close range and often in mixed light — daylight from a window, warm indoor lighting, a photographer's flash. The skin has to read as skin in all three. That means buildable coverage instead of a flat mask, careful work around the eyes so they stay open and bright across a long ceremony, and a lip that survives the signing, the hugging and the first meal without constant reapplication.

Included in a Nikaah booking

  • Skin preparation — cleanse, prep and prime matched to your skin type and the length of the day
  • Long-wear base — coverage chosen to photograph naturally in daylight, indoor light and flash
  • Soft defined eyes — a refined eye with lashes, scaled to your comfort rather than to a trend
  • Dupatta-aware finish — the look is set with the draping and pinning in mind so nothing shifts
  • Touch-up kit — your lip colour and blotting essentials to carry through the ceremony

Nikaah makeup questions

Will Nikaah makeup look too heavy in photographs?

No. A Nikaah look is deliberately lighter than a Baraat or reception look. The products are chosen for close-range photography, so the skin still reads as skin while the coverage holds through the ceremony.

Can you do Nikaah makeup at home or at the masjid?

Yes. All makeup is done on location. Aisha travels from Iselin, NJ to your home, hotel or ceremony venue; share the address when you enquire so timings and travel can be confirmed.

Can family members be done at the same appointment?

Yes. Makeup for mothers, sisters and other family members can be added to the booking and scheduled around the bride so nobody is rushed.
